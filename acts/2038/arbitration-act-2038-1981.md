# Arbitration Act, 2038 (1981)

↔ Arbitration Act, 2038 (1981) Date of Royal Seal and Publication in the Nepal Gazette

2038.7. 28 (Nov.13, 1981)

An Act made to provide for Arbitration

Preamble: Whereas, it is expedient to make legal provision relating to arbitration.

Now, therefore, be it enacted by His Majesty King Bir Bikram Shah Dev, on the advice and with the consent of the Rastriya Panchayat.

#### 1. Short title and commencement

(1) This Act may be called the "Arbitration Act, 2038 (1981)"

(2) This Act shall come into force on such Date as His Majesty's Government may by Notification published in the Nepal Gazette, appoint.

#### 2. Definition

Unless the subject or context otherwise requires, in this Act,-

(a) "Agreement" means a contract concluded at between any Two parties for the settlement of any present or future dispute through an arbitrator with or without indicating the name of the Arbitrator.

(b) "Court of Appeal" means the Court of Appeal of the area where arbitration is initiated.

(c) "District Court" means any one of the following District Courts:

(1) The District Court of such district as may be specified by the parties seeking arbitration for the propose of arbitration, and

(d) "Arbitrator" also includes the Chief Arbitrator.

(e) "Award" means an Arbitration Award.

(f ) "Party" means any of the parties to an arbitration.

#### 3. Disputes which can be Arbitrated Upon

Any dispute under a bilateral or multilateral agreement may be settled through arbitration in the manner stipulated therein.

#### 4. Number of Arbitrators

(1) Unless otherwise stipulated as to the number of arbitrators in the agreement, it shall be deemed to have referred to a sole arbitrator.

(2) In case the arbitrator to be appointed under an agreement is in even number, the arbitrators shall appoint one additional arbitrator so as to turn the number of arbitrators uneven.

(3) If there are more than one arbitrators, the chief arbitrator shall be designated in accordance with the provision contained in the agreement, if any, and if the agreement does not contain any such provision the arbitratorchosen by the arbitrators shall be the chief arbitrator.

#### 5. Procedure for appointment of arbitrator

(1) The arbitrator shall be appointed according to the procedure laid down in the agreement if it is stipulated therein.

(2) If no arbitrator can be appointed by following the procedure laid down under the agreement or if the agreement does not lay such procedure, any of the parties to the agreement may file an application to the District Court requesting for the appointment of an arbitrator.

<sup>2</sup>www.lawcommission.gov.np (3) If the arbitrator is to be appointed by the District Court, it (District Court) shall, as far as possible, consult the parties and if there is unanimity among the parties to nominate a person, the District Court shall be required to appoint such a person as an arbitrator. If the parties fail to reach unanimity in the course of such consultation, the District Court shall appoint such a person as an arbitrator as it may deem appropriate.

#### 6. Panel of Arbitrators to be maintained

For the purpose of Sub-section (3) of Section 5, every District Court may maintain a Panel of Arbitrators showing their full names, occupations and addresses and consisting of such persons as may be considered qualified to become arbitrators on the recommendation of such a local organization or agency as may be connected with trade or industry or of the local Panchayat.

Provided that, no appointment of a person as arbitrator shall be invalidated merely on the ground that his/her name is not included in such a panel.

#### 7. Appointment of Arbitrator under special circumstances

(1) In case an arbitrator refuses to act or dies or is removed by the Court or is unable to act due to some other reason, the vacancy so caused shall be filled up by appointing another arbitrator within Fifteen days from the date of falling vacancy in the manner in which such arbitrator had originally been appointed.

(2) In case no arbitrator is appointed within the time limit stipulated in Sub-section (1), any arbitrator or any party may file an application before the District Court, and the District Court shall appoint another arbitrator within Fifteen days from the date of filing such application.

#### 8. Disqualification to become an Arbitrator

No person, either disqualified to execute contract under the Contract Act, 2023 (1966) or any

<sup>3</sup>www.lawcommission.gov.np other prevailing law, or who has any interest in matters relating to arbitration, shall be eligible to become an arbitrator.

#### 9. Circumstances in which an Arbitrator may be removed

(1) Any party may submit an application to the District Court requesting for the removal of an arbitrator on one or more of the following grounds:

(a) That an arbitrator has failed to act impartially.

(b) That an arbitrator has acted improperly in course of arbitration proceeding or

(c) That an arbitrator has any hidden interest to be arbitrated upon is biased towards any party.

(2) The District Court may issue order for the removal of an arbitrator if the particulars contained in the application under Sub-section (1) are found to be correct.

(3) No party shall be entitled to remove any arbitrator except in the circumstances referred to in Sub-section (1).

#### 10. Office of the Arbitrator

The office of the arbitrator shall be located,-

(a) At the place mentioned in the agreement, if so specified.

(b) If no place is specified in the agreement, at the place, selected by the parties, and

(c) If the parties fail to select such place within a reasonable period, or in case there is no unanimity among the parties over the location, at the place designated by the arbitrator.

#### 11. Claims or counter-claims to be filed

(1) Any party seeking arbitration shall be required to file his/her claim to the arbitrator with full particulars of the dispute that has arisen and of the action that it expects to be taken within Three months of the date on which such a dispute has arisen, if the

<sup>4</sup>www.lawcommission.gov.np arbitrator is named in the agreement, otherwise within Three months of the appointment of an arbitrator, if the is to be appointed after the dispute so arises.

(2) In case any claim is filed under Sub-section (1), the arbitrator shall furnish a copy thereof to the other party and allow him/her a period of Thirty days for filing the counter-claims.

(3) A copy of the agreement and such other documents that had been executed before or after entering into agreement shall be required to produce while filing claims or counter-claims pursuant to Sub-sections (1) or (2).

(4) In case any of the parties wants to substantiate any of its claims through a witness, he/she shall have to mention the full name and address of such witness in the claims or counterclaims to be filed pursuant to Sub-section (1) or (2) and such party shall be required to produce its witness before the arbitrator on such date as the arbitrator may appoint.

(5) Certified copies of original documents may be submitted and the arbitrator may as and when necessary, direct the submission of such original documents.

(6) In case no claims are filed under Sub-section (1), the appointment of arbitrator shall stand ipso facto cancelled, and the party shall not be entitled to initiate any action in the future in respect of such claims.

#### 12. Cancellation of Record of Cases filed with a Court

In case the parties to a case pending at a court, which otherwise can be settled through compromise under prevailing law, file a petition to the court requesting for the leave of the court to have the dispute settled through arbitration, the

<sup>5</sup>www.lawcommission.gov.np court may order the cancellation of the case from its record and discontinuation of the parties from appearing before the court.

Provided that, the court shall not order the cancellation of such a case from its record in the following circumstances:

(a) If circumstances arise in which a decision is to be taken on matters which could be arbitrated upon and as also on those which cannot be arbitrated upon.

(b) If no valid reason is found for referring such a dispute to arbitration.

#### 13. Cases not to be filed with a Court

(1) Save as otherwise provided in this Act, no case may be filed with a court in any matter requiring arbitration.

(2) After an arbitration award has been pronounced, no suit in respect of the award may be filed with a court in such a way as to affect such award adversely except in the circumstances where it may be reversed according to this Act.

#### 14. Circumstances in which Award need not be made

(1) The arbitrator shall not be required to make an award on any matter which may be inextricably linked with such matter which may not be arbitrated upon.

(2) In case circumstances so arise that arbitrator is debarred to make any decision pursuant to Sub-section (1), he shall ask the concerned party to file complaints with a court, and if any of the parties is absent, a notice thereof shall be served to him/her. Such party shall be required to file a complaint with a court within Thirty Five days from the date of being informed or of the receipt of such notice.

#### 16. Representation by Proxy

Any party may have represented itself by proxy in the course of arbitration proceeding and the proceeding for the execution of an award.

#### 17. Powers of an Arbitrator

The power of an arbitrator shall be:

(a) To direct the parties to appear before him/her, cause to produce documents, and to record statements as may be necessary.

(b) To record the statements of witnesses under administration of oath.

(c) To solicit opinion of experts.

(d) To issue certified copies of documents.

(e) If any of the parties is a foreign national, to accept such amount of security as may be required for the execution of the award made by the arbitrator, and have it deposited with a bank, or accept a bond.

(f) To issue such interim orders as it deems necessary.

(g) If the parties have vested him with a specific power, to exercise the same.

(h) To make an award conditional or alternative

#### 18. Award of the Arbitrator

(1) If there are more than Two arbitrators, the opinion of the majority shall be deemed to be the decision of the arbitrators.

(2) If the arbitrators fail to reach to a decision by reason of having difference of opinion of respective arbitrators shall be referred to the persons mentioned hereunder and the opinion endorsed by such person shall be treated as the decision of the arbitrator

(b) Where no prevailing law or any agreement provides for any such provision, to the Chief Judge of the Court of Appeal or any other Judge of the same Court that may be designated by the Chief Judge.

#### 19. Facts to be expressed in an Award

(1) An arbitrator shall be required to express the following particulars in an arbitration award:

(a) Brief particulars of the dispute referred to for arbitration.

(b) The findings of the arbitrator and the reasons on which he/she has based for writing at such findings.

(c) Matters pertaining to realization of claims, or Compensation or the amount thereof.

(2) The arbitrator shall be required to initial every page of the award and put his full signature with his full name, address and date, on the last page.

(3) The arbitrator shall be required to read out the arbitration award in the presence of the parties, and secure the signature of those who have heard the award. In case any party is absent at the time of reading out award, a notice along-with a copy of the award shall be sent to such party.

#### 20. A ward not subject to review

An arbitrator, once he/she has made his/his award on the dispute referred to him/her, shall not review it and make fresh award.

Provided that, this provision shall not be deemed to have prevented him/her from correcting minor errors of writing, or insert certain omitted particulars, without prejudice to the substances of such award.

Circumstances in which an award may be Invalidated : (1) A party to a case may file a petition, along-with relevant documents and a copy of the award with the Court of Appeal requesting it to invalidate the award within Thirty Five days of hearing such award or receipt of a notice there of under Sub-section(3) of section 19.

(a) Biased or mala fide,

(b) Secured in a fraudulent manner, or under duress or undue influence.

(c) Against any prevailing law,

(d) Inherent with apparent legal errors,

(e) Vague and meaningless,

(f) Against any of the conditions stipulated in the agreement, or

(g) Based on wrong principle.

#### 22. Enforcement of an Award

Every party shall be required to enforce an award within Sixty days of its making.

#### 23. Execution of Award by Court

In case an award is not enforced under Section 22, the concerted party may file a petition with the District Court within Thirty days from the date of the expire of the time limit stipulated for such enforcement and when such a petition is submitted, the District

<sup>9</sup>www.lawcommission.gov.np Court shall execute such an award, as if it were its own judgment, ordinarily within Thirty days of filling such petition.

#### 24. Enforcement of an Award made in a Foreign Country

(1) Any agreement where a person residing in the Kingdom of Nepal is a party and there is a provision to provide for arbitration in any foreign country, an award is made pursuant to such an agreement, the award so made in accordance with the law of that foreign country may be executed in the Kingdom of Nepal if-

(a) The arbitrator had been appointed in accordance with the provision of the agreement or the law of that country where the award had been granted.

(b) The parties had been informed of the proceedings in time.

(c) The award is based solely on the conditions stipulated in the agreement, and

(d) The decision is final under the law of the country where such an award is made.

(2) Any party requesting for the enforcement of the foreign arbitral award in the Kingdom of Nepal shall be required to submit a petition before the Court of Appeal. If the Court of Appeal is satisfied that the conditions mentioned under Sub-section (1) have been fulfilled, it (Court of Appeal) shall send the petition to the District Court directing it to execute such award.

(3) After it has received the award under Sub-section (2), the District Court shall execute it as if it were its own judgment ordinarily within Thirty days of its receipt.

#### 25. Enforcement of Arbitration made under International Treaty, Agreement or Arrangement

Any arbitration, made either under any international treaty or agreement to which His Majesty Government is a party or under any such international arrangement relating to arbitration which His Majesty's Government may has adopted, shall be deemed to have been effected pursuant to this Act and shall be enforced in accordance with this Act.

#### 26. Cost of Arbitration Proceedings

(1) While submitting documents to an arbitrator, the parties seeking arbitration shall also submit to him/her a reasonable amount as the cost for arbitration proceedings. Such amount shall be utilized to meet the necessary cost involved in the arbitration proceedings.

(2) If the amount received under Sub-section (1) is not sufficient to meet the cost, the arbitrator may collect additional amount to meet the cost from the respective parties on an equal basis and in case of surplus, the surplus amount shall be refunded to the respective parties.

#### 27. Statement of Arbitration Proceedings and Arbitrator's Remuneration

(1) An arbitrator shall prepare a statement explicitly indicating the work performed each day of arbitration proceeding and the actual number of hours utilized in doing so and attach it to the case file.

(2) If the agreement is silent as to the amount of remuneration to be paid to arbitrators, every arbitrator shall be entitled to obtain remuneration at the rate of Two Hundred Fifty Rupees for each day of work done in

<sup>11</sup>www.lawcommission.gov.np respect of arbitration from respective parties on a proportionate basis either at the time of making the award or thereafter.

(3) Notwithstanding anything contained in Sub-section (2), if a separate agreement as to remuneration exists between the parties and the arbitrator, remuneration shall be paid in accordance with such agreement.

(4) In case the arbitrator is unable to obtain his remuneration from the respective parties, he shall submit a petition to the District Court and upon receipt of such a petition the District Court shall proceed to recover the remuneration payable to such arbitrator in the manner as it may undertake to recover its fines.

#### 28. Name and Address to be furnished

(1) For the purpose of facilitating correspondence or contacts, the parties and arbitrators shall proved their names and addresses to each other.

(2) In the course of arbitration proceeding, contacts may be maintained between the parties, or between the arbitrator and the concerned party by means of registered postal letters.

#### 29. Case file to be kept safely

(1) Every arbitrator shall maintain a case file and attach thereto, according to its serial number, the statements recorded from the parties and the documents and evidence received from them and also mention the date and time of doing so.

(2) After the completion of arbitration proceeding, every case file shall be sent to the District Court for safe custody.

#### 30. Right and Liabilities to be transferred

In case any party dies, or disppears, or becomes insane, all his/her rights and liabilities shall be transferred to such of his/her claimants who may be entitled to inherit his/her property under prevailing law.

#### 32. Prevailing Law to apply

Where there is any special provision in any prevailing law in respect to arbitration, it shall be followed accordingly and in other cases the provisions of this Act shall be applicable.

Provided that, parties may mutually agree, if they so wish, to have the provisions of this Act applied in their arbitration proceedings instead of the special provisions relating to arbitration contained in any prevailing law.
